## (c) Differences from the existing partial transcription

The existing draft (letters.json body + L108_transcription.md) needs substantial correction,
including its "established" first paragraph:

1. First paragraph: existing reads "Received four of your letters & stay over with
   twenty-two of them this week... (Bunny letters?)". The scan actually reads "Received ten
   of your letters & they sure were very interesting even though they were a little late."
   There is no "twenty-two", no "Bunny letters", no "stay over" in this letter. The rest of
   the old first paragraph ("I just love to get them... write as often as you possible can")
   is a paraphrase-level misreading of "Joan, you can write such nice letters & I just love
   to get them... Anyway I know you will always write to me. (Want you?)".
2. Existing bracketed guess "[[Joan, I'm so glad through with that, Carroll's joined the
   navy. He will regret it I am sure.]]" is a misreading. The actual passage: it is TOMMY
   who "got into the Air Corps instead" (bound for Texas); CARROLL wrote saying he'd like to
   be out there with Gene; and EARL "has gone by now" — Gene wished Earl "had stay out of
   this for he's younger enough to never have to go." Nobody "will regret it I am sure" —
   that sentence does not appear.
3. Existing line "Joan, even if your Carroll's old enough to be there with me..." is a
   garbled composite of the Carroll sentence above; it should be dropped.
4. Entirely new content recovered: Joan's recent operation (twice), the summer-vacation
   passage, the ring Gene sent Joan (Martha's and his mother's reactions), the birthday
   wishes, Gene's joke that he'll need an operation himself before long, and the full
   closing.
5. Signature: currently "[Gene]"; actually signed "Lots & Lots of love with K[isses]. /
   always / (Eugene Lankford) ((Gene))".
6. The note in letters.json ("twenty-two of Joan's letters arriving in one week",
   "Carroll had just joined the Navy... 'He will regret it I am sure'") repeats the

## (d) Evidence about the year

- Envelope: circular "PASSED BY NAVAL CENSOR" stamp with censor's ink initials (looks like
  "HBM"/"ABM") — wartime, so 1942 at the earliest. U.S. NAVY machine cancel reads "MAY 12"
  with NO year slug visible. 6-cent red Transport airmail stamp (issued mid-1941) —
  consistent. Addressed "Miss Joan Northcutt, Stanford, Kentucky, Route 1", return
  "Eugene Lankford, U.S.S. New Orleans"; "Air Mail" underlined.
- Strongest content tie to 1942: "I received two letter from, Martha, saying she was much
  better." L107 (July 4, dated 1942) says Martha "seems to be improving very fast now. I
  know though that sickness she had really hurt her very much." Same recovery arc, a few
  weeks apart — L108 sits naturally in the same year as L107.
- Early-war enlistment wave: Tommy just accepted into the Air Corps (training in Texas),
  Earl just gone into service, Carroll writing wistfully about joining Gene — this cluster
  of hometown boys entering the services fits spring 1942, not a later year.
- L107 (July 4, 1942) also mentions "guess, Carroll is home for his vacation now" —
  consistent with Carroll still a civilian in May, as L108 implies (he only *wishes* he
  were out there), and contradicting the old note's claim that Carroll had already joined.
- Joan's birthday: Gene stops to wish her "a very very happy birthday" — supports the May
  date (letter written shortly before/around her birthday); no year information by itself.
- Nothing in the letter contradicts 1942; nothing (no place names, no battle references —
  censorship held) positively proves it. Verdict: 1942 remains the best inference, now
  reinforced by the Martha-illness cross-reference with L107.
