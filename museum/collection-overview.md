# Collection Overview

Prepared for The National WWII Museum. This answers the museum's remaining
intake questions about the material itself: its contents, condition, and
provenance.

## What the collection contains

Approximately **110 items**, chiefly letters written by Raymond Eugene "Gene"
Lankford to Joan Northcutt between **April 1940 and 1943**. The collection also
includes:

- The **original envelopes**, most with the letters still folded inside them.
- A small number of related pieces: a Christmas card, a Postal Telegraph
  holiday telegram, two envelopes whose letters are lost, and two letters
  written **to** Gene by Navy friends (Don Chapin and the wife of his shipmate
  Jack Parkhurst) after his discharge.

## Condition

Generally **very good**. The letters are folded in their original envelopes;
the paper is sound and the ink legible. Damage is limited to occasional minor
tearing and fading. One often-photographed letter (December 1941) has a small
tear at the upper left.

## Provenance

The letters were written from Gene's Navy stations — Great Lakes, Pearl
Harbor, the West Coast, and at sea — to Joan at Route 1, Stanford, Kentucky.
Joan kept them for the rest of her life. After her death in 1992 they remained
with the family and were rediscovered years later by Gene and Joan's daughter,
**Elizabeth "Libby" Lankford Morris**, in a box in a storage building behind
the family home in Somerset, Kentucky.

## Related materials the family can share

- **Photographs of the collection** (envelopes and sample letters), already
  shared with the museum in 2022, plus higher-resolution page scans made in
  2026.
- An **oral-history video** of Gene recounting a wartime story, recorded by the
  family.
- A digital edition of the full collection — transcriptions, page images, and a
  "cast of characters" — published at
  **blakewmorrisuk.github.io/love-always-gene**.
- A compiled **biographical and ship-history research document** (about 25
  pages).
