# Biographies

Prepared for The National WWII Museum as donor-provided interpretive material,
in response to the museum's request for "biographies with details on birth,
family life, education, service, marriage, postwar career."

> Note for the family: these notes draw on a compiled research document and on
> the letters themselves. A few dates (the marriage date of April 20, 1943, and
> the exact discharge date) should be confirmed against primary records before
> they are treated as final.

## Raymond Eugene "Gene" Lankford (1921–2008)

**Birth and family.** Raymond Eugene Lankford, known all his life as Gene, was
born on November 21, 1921, in Stanford, Lincoln County, Kentucky, a son of
William Porter Lankford and Marguerite Lynn Lankford. He grew up in a large
family. An older brother, Carroll, had already served in the Navy by the time
Gene enlisted; his brother Tom and other siblings appear throughout the
letters.

**Service.** Gene enlisted in the United States Navy in the spring of 1940, at
eighteen, and left Kentucky for recruit training at the U.S. Naval Training
Station at Great Lakes, Illinois. He was then assigned to the heavy cruiser
**U.S.S. New Orleans (CA-32)**, serving in her **F Division**. The New Orleans
was moored at Pearl Harbor on the morning of December 7, 1941. Over the
following year she fought through the Coral Sea, Midway, and the campaign for
Guadalcanal. On the night of November 30, 1942, off Tassafaronga in the
Solomon Islands, a Japanese torpedo struck the New Orleans and tore away
roughly one hundred and fifty feet of her bow, killing 183 of her crew. Most
of Joan's letters to Gene, kept aboard the ship, were lost with the bow. Gene
was honorably discharged in 1943.

**Marriage and postwar life.** On April 20, 1943, Gene married Joan Northcutt,
the young woman he had courted across four years of letters. He settled in
Somerset, Kentucky, where for many years he owned and operated Crest
Electronics, and was a member of High Street Baptist Church. He and Joan were
married for forty-nine years, until her death in 1992; he later married Betty
Pitman Lankford. Gene died at home in Somerset on May 23, 2008, at the age of
eighty-six, and was buried with military honors at Lakeside Memorial Gardens.
He was survived by his children Raymond Eugene Lankford Jr., Robert Lee
Lankford, and Elizabeth "Libby" Lankford Morris, and was predeceased by his son
William Joe Lankford and by two infant daughters, Debra Ann and Janie Wood.

## Joan Northcutt Lankford (1925–1992)

**Birth and family.** Joan Northcutt was born on May 17, 1925, and grew up on
Route 1 outside Stanford, Kentucky.

**During the war.** Joan was a schoolgirl in Stanford when Gene left for the
Navy in 1940 — fourteen years old. Gene's letters follow her through her
exams and her grades and the small news of a Kentucky town at the edge of a
war. For the length of the war she remained at home in Stanford while Gene was
at sea, and it was to her, at Stanford, that nearly every letter in this
collection was addressed.

**Marriage and after.** Joan married Gene on April 20, 1943, and they were
together forty-nine years. She kept every letter Gene wrote her for the rest of
her life, and in later years would not let her own daughter read them, saying
they were too personal. Joan died on November 19, 1992, and is buried at
Lakeside Memorial Gardens in Somerset. The letters were found years after her
death, in a box in a storage building behind the family home.
